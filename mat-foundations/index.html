Raft/Mat Foundation Design in Burlington: Ground-Level Engineering

Geotechnical engineering with regional judgment.

LEARN MORE

In Burlington, many of the older established neighborhoods sit on thick sequences of glaciolacustrine silty clay — the Halton Till doesn't show up uniformly across the city, and that changes everything for a shallow footing. We design raft foundations when the bearing stratum varies more than 20% over a single building footprint, or when total settlement estimates exceed 25 mm differential. A raft isn't just a thick slab — it's a structural mat that bridges soft spots and limits angular distortion. Before finalizing mat geometry, we often run a few CPT soundings to map the compressible layer thickness across the lot, and pair that with lab consolidation curves from Shelby tube samples. That way the reinforcement layout matches what the ground actually needs, not what a textbook assumes.

A raft foundation in Burlington clay isn't about bearing capacity — it's a settlement control problem, pure and simple.

Process overview

Burlington sits at about 74 m above Lake Ontario, but the real story is the buried bedrock valleys — the Dundas Valley influence creates localized depressions in the rock surface filled with up to 30 m of soft sediment. We see this directly in borehole logs near the QEW corridor. A raft foundation here has to handle edge conditions where one corner bears on stiff till and the opposite corner floats on compressible silt. Our design approach uses modulus of subgrade reaction values we back-calculate from field SPT drilling data — not generic tables. For the concrete design, CSA A23.3 governs the flexural and punching shear checks, and we specify a minimum 400 mm thickness with two-way reinforcement unless the geotechnical report demands otherwise. We also look hard at frost protection: Burlington's frost depth is 1.2 m per the Ontario Building Code, so the raft edge beam must extend below that unless we insulate horizontally with rigid XPS.

Local context

We use a total station and a digital inclinometer on the rebar cage before the first concrete pour — if the reinforcement is off by even 30 mm vertically, the internal lever arm shrinks and you lose moment capacity right where the raft spans over a soft pocket. The biggest headache in Burlington is undetected organics or buried topsoil lenses within the Halton Till. A raft bridges small anomalies, but a 400 mm thick mat won't survive a 1.5 m deep decomposed root mat under the northeast corner. Differential settlement can exceed 40 mm within two years, cracking partition walls and binding doors. That's why we insist on at least three boreholes per building footprint and a thorough visual log of the auger cuttings. If we find compressible organics below 3 m depth, we sometimes switch from a raft to stone columns as ground improvement before placing the mat, or deepen the edge beams into competent till. Skipping that extra borehole costs a lot more than the drilling.

Top questions

When is a raft foundation better than individual footings in Burlington?

When the bearing stratum is variable within the building footprint, or when isolated footings would cover more than 50% of the plan area. It is also the right call when total settlement estimates exceed 25 mm differential — the raft bridges soft spots and reduces angular distortion.

How long does a typical raft foundation design take?

From the first borehole to the stamped structural drawings, budget three to four weeks. The geotechnical fieldwork takes a few days, lab consolidation tests need about a week, and the design and drafting take the remainder.

What does a raft/mat foundation design cost for a Burlington home?

For a typical single-family home, the combined geotechnical investigation and structural raft design ranges from CA$1.380 to CA$6.070, depending on the number of boreholes and the complexity of the reinforcement detailing required.

Do you handle the frost protection design for the raft edge?

Yes. The edge beam is detailed to extend at least 1.2 m below finished grade per the Ontario Building Code, or we design a horizontal insulation layout with rigid XPS to prevent frost heave under the mat.

Can a raft foundation be used on a sloped lot in Burlington?

It can, but it needs stepped edge beams or a deepened perimeter wall on the downhill side. The key is maintaining the minimum frost depth at all points while keeping the top of slab level — we detail the transition with vertical rebar dowels between the steps.
